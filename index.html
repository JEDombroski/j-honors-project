Uncovering the Queer History of St. Kate's

J Dombroski

How have opinions about LGBTQ+ issues at St. Kate's changed over time?

How have they differed between students, faculty, and administration?

St. Catherine University’s identities as a Catholic, women’s, and social justice oriented institution have long shaped the attitudes of its students, staff, faculty, and administration. For LGBTQ+ community members, these intersecting commitments represent an important part of their experiences at St. Kate’s, as they generate both conditions of support and tension for LGBTQ+ visibility on campus.

The St. Catherine University archives are host to over 300 documents, images, news articles, and other materials tracing the school’s history of queer visibility and resistance, covering a diversity of topics from queer student organization events to censored letters between a well known CSJ and an openly bisexual poet.

This digital exhibit uses some of these archival materials to portray two distinct moments of tension between students and administration: conflict over sidewalk chalking policies in the 1990s and debates over trans inclusivity continuing into the present day.
